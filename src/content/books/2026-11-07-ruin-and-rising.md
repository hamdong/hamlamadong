When I started the Shadow and Bone trilogy I didn't know what I was getting myself in to, yet here I am five books later (counting Six of Crows and Crooked Kingdom) and it's got to be one of--if not my favourite--fantasy series I've ever read. Ruin and Rising is the third book in the series, but I'm using it to represent the first three books as a whole.

The story follows Alina Starkov, a teenage girl who finds herself turned into a Grisha. Grishas are basically people in this universe who are gifted with supernatural powers (or the "small-science" as the book so keenly calls it). Think of people who can control elements, like fire, wind, and water, or folks with the ability to heal wounds or bend metal. There's a specific classification for each power (for example, fire-wielders are naturally called "Inferni"), but our main character Alina has the unique power of controlling light. No one else in the world has this power, and fittingly enough, she ends up having to clash with the only other dark-wielder in this universe, a teenage boy known as the Darkling. This forms the crux of the conflict in the three books, as the Darkling plans to free the Grisha (otherwise known as Ravkans) from being enslaved by every other region in the world by becoming its king and enveloping the world in darkness. There's more to it than that but that is the gist of it.

There is a bit of romance sprinkled throughout the series as Alina must team up with her childhood best-friend Mal, a boy who grew up with her at the orphanage they were raised in. Mal is a skilled tracker and hunter, and ends up becoming vital to Alina's mission as across all three books they have to hunt down three special ancient animals who can supposedly amplify the power of Alina's "sun summoning," which she will need to defeat the Darkling, as well as this massive black fog cutting across the country called the Fold.

Along the way we run into several interesting characters who either help Alina on her quest or become some kind of obstacle for her to overcome. Some of the more memorable ones for me include Nikolai, Genya, Zoya, Tolya, Tamar, David, and Sergei. By the end of the third book the characters really form a sense of camaraderie between one other after going through hell and back just to try and defeat the Darkling. Maybe some of the story was a bit predictable, but it was definitely sincere and honest with itself. I really enjoyed the adventure overall.

Compared to the future books I read for this series, the writing in the original trilogy seem to be a bit of a downgrade, but it's expected as the author was much younger in her career when she started it. Honestly, I'd say it's held up pretty well, and it's also a pretty easy read because of the style in which it's written. It forms a good introductory to the world, as things feel simpler compared to what would go on in Six of Crows. Still, I thoroughly enjoyed it and I'm glad to have finally acquired a physical copy of these for myself. I would definitely recommend it to anyone wanting a fun adventure in a fantasy world with a bit of romance mixed in.
