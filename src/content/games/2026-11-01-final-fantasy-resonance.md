If there was any one game to take me by surprise this year it was Final Fantasy Resonance. This is a game that hearkens back to the franchise's 2D days on the Super Famicom, complete with a cast of original characters embarking on a quest to save the world from the dreaded Sworn Six as they attempt to throw the world out of balance by destroying its Crystals. In this world, the player's party will travel to many distinct lands, battling monsters along the way, finding secrets, and uncovering the mystery of Arcwitch Fina, shown on the cover of the box. I'm more than halfway through the main campaign of the game and I gotta say at this point, I feel confident is calling this my game of the year for 2026.

The game follows a traditional Final Fantasy format from customizing the party right down to the combat system. Similar to a lot of modern turn-based RPGs, this game reintroduces the "break" mechanic that allows enemies to be staggered, leaving wide openings for characters to dominate the battlefield. Certain enemies have strengths and weaknesses to certain elements, which you can customize on the characters through the use of the newly introduced Vision system (more on that later). Anyone whose played the OG Final Fantasies (namely 1-6 although the same patterns can be seen in some of the early 3D games) will feel a sense of familiarity here. There's status effects, physical vs magical attacks, single-target vs AOE spells, items, and many other factors that go in to the outcome of a battle. Each combat scenario feels different from the last, and with over 60 hours of a campaign to get through, suffice to say there is plenty of content to sink your teeth into.

The big claim to fame for this game are the Visions, which are essentially spirits of characters from previous Final Fantasy games that let you customize your characters beyond their designated "jobs." The Warrior of Light, for example, comes with multiple abilities for sustaining damage, such as a counterattack skill, and the Cure spell. This makes him synergize well with bulky characters, but nothing prevents you from equipping him to a damage-dealing character or support character. There's really no limitation here as the game encourages you to experiment with different combinations and skills. No two play-throughs may be the same, and I gotta say it's definitely the most fun aspect of the game for me. I just like going with wacky builds and sending them in to battle to see how things go. I've been playing the Expert difficulty setting (the current highest setting) and while I've run into some stoppers here and there, a little tweaking in the strategy helps me overcome whatever boss fight is standing in my way. It's been tremendously satisfying in that regard.

The artwork, the music, the presentation as a whole is so clean and beautiful that I find myself taking a pause and just staring at the screen sometimes, admiring the perfect-pixelated environment of the towns, cities, and outdoor areas. Enemy sprites, effects, and even just the lighting have so much attention to detail put in to them that it's hard not to notice them. From the shimmering water effects to the sunlight coming in from windows in buildings, I must say this has to be one of the prettiest-looking games in my Switch catalogue. It really goes to show the artistic integrity of 2D sprite-work, as 100 years from now this game will look just as beautiful as the day that it launched. Square Enix have truly outdone themselves with this.

I'm really looking forward to where this adventure will go--I almost don't want it to end. That's when I know I'm so in love with a game is when I strive to see every aspect of it, taking in as much as I can to the point of addiction. It doesn't happen to me with most games I play, but when it does, that's when I know I've found a winner.
